How to Use SonicCanvas

🎤 1. Getting Started

Step 1 — Launch SonicCanvas and tap the central Mic button. Allow microphone access when prompted — the app needs it to turn live sound into painting.
Step 2 — Make some sound: talk, sing, play music, or just let the app listen to your surroundings. The canvas starts painting immediately, and the mini waveform at the top shows the live audio input.
Step 3 — Tap the Mic button again to stop listening and freeze the painting.
💡 Tip: Different sounds paint differently — bass drums bloom deep colors, voices add mid-range strokes, and cymbals sprinkle bright highlights.

🎨 2. Choosing a Painting Style

Tap Styles to pick from 8 painting styles, switchable at any time without interrupting your session:

  • Watercolor — fluid pigment that spreads with volume and deepens with bass
  • Oil Painting — thick strokes; pitch shifts the tone, loudness builds texture
  • Abstract — a Fourier-style stack of harmonic rings driven by the live frequency spectrum
  • Impressionism — shimmering color dabs on a warm canvas ground
  • Sketch — graphite cross-hatching that darkens as the sound gets louder
  • Graffiti — spray paint and drips on a concrete wall
  • Neon — glowing waves whose shapes are built from the audio spectrum
  • Line Art — minimalist contour lines rippling with every harmonic

The same panel also lets you set the canvas size: Full, 1:1, 9:16, or 16:9.

🖌 3. Paint Modes and Canvas Control

  • Cumulative mode (stack icon) — strokes layer up over time into a finished artwork
  • Pulse mode (bolt icon) — the canvas refreshes in real time with the sound, great for music visualization
  • Eraser button — clears the canvas and starts fresh
  • Pinch & drag — zoom up to 4× and pan to inspect brush detail; double-tap to reset
  • Tap the canvas — toggles fullscreen, hiding all controls for distraction-free painting

📷 4. Capturing Images

  1. Tap Capture at any moment to save the current painting.
  2. Images are exported at your chosen quality (1080P or 4K, set in Settings → Export Quality).
  3. Each capture is saved to your in-app Gallery and (with your permission) to the iOS Photos app.

🎬 5. Recording Videos

  1. Tap Record — the controls fade out and recording starts on a clean, fullscreen canvas.
  2. Create your art; the video also captures the ambient sound driving the painting.
  3. Tap anywhere to bring the controls back, then tap Stop.
  4. The video tail is automatically trimmed so no buttons appear in your final clip, which is saved to the Gallery and Photos.

🖼 6. Managing Your Gallery

  • Open the Gallery from the top-left button to browse all saved images and videos
  • Tap any artwork to preview it, rename it, check its details, or share it
  • Share Art Card — generates a 9:16 branded story card sized for Instagram and TikTok
  • Use Select for batch deletion, and the search bar to find works by title or style

⚙️ 7. Customizing Settings

  • Audio Sensitivity — Low / Medium / High response to sound
  • Export Quality — 1080P or 4K canvas resolution
  • Color Scheme — Brand, Warm, Cool, Morandi, Neon, or fully Custom colors
  • Dynamic Range, Brush Size, Brush Diffusion — fine-tune how boldly the audio paints
  • Theme — Light, Dark, or follow the system
  • Storage & Clear Cache — see space used by your works and clear everything if needed

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does SonicCanvas ask for microphone permission?

Microphone access is essential for the core feature — analyzing live sound (volume, frequency, pitch, timbre) and turning it into painting in real time. All analysis happens locally on your device, and the audio stream is not stored. You can revoke the permission anytime in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone.

Is my audio recorded or uploaded anywhere?

No. During painting the audio is analyzed in memory and immediately discarded. The only time sound is saved is when you explicitly record a video of your session — and that video stays on your device. Nothing is ever uploaded to any server.

The painting barely reacts to sound. What should I do?

Raise Audio Sensitivity to High and Dynamic Range in Settings, move the sound source closer to the microphone, and make sure the mini waveform at the top is moving. Cumulative mode also needs a moment of sustained sound before strokes become visible.

What's the difference between Cumulative and Pulse mode?

Cumulative mode keeps every stroke, layering them into a finished artwork over time — clear the canvas with the eraser button when you want to start over. Pulse mode redraws the canvas live with the sound, which looks great with rhythmic music.

Does SonicCanvas work with Bluetooth or external microphones?

Yes. The app follows your device's active audio input, including Bluetooth headsets and wired/USB microphones.

My recorded video is missing or failed to save.

Make sure screen recording isn't restricted (iOS Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions) and that no other app is recording the screen at the same time. If an error alert appears, the message describes the cause — feel free to email it to us.

Where are my artworks stored?

All images and videos live on your device in the app's local work library, with optional copies in your iOS Photos app. Deleting the app removes the work library, but copies saved to Photos remain.

Can I use SonicCanvas offline?

Yes! SonicCanvas works completely offline. All audio analysis and painting happens on-device. No network connection is required.

Does SonicCanvas collect any personal data?

No. SonicCanvas collects no personal information, uses no analytics, and shows no ads. See our Privacy Policy for details.

Why did the canvas clear when I changed style or canvas size?

Each style paints on its own background (dark canvas vs. paper), and each canvas size uses its own resolution, so switching restarts the canvas. Capture your artwork first if you want to keep it.
