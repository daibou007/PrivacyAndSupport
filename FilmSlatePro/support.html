How to Use FilmSlate Pro

🎬 1. The Slate and Its Fields

The main screen is a full-screen digital clapperboard that the camera can film directly. The board shows these fields:

  • PRODUCTION — Production name (e.g., "The Midnight Shoot")
  • ROLL — Roll or magazine identifier (e.g., A001)
  • SCENE — Scene number or identifier
  • SHOT — Shot number within the scene
  • TAKE — Take number (auto-increments with each clap)
  • DIRECTOR — Director's name
  • CAMERAMAN — Camera operator or cinematographer
  • DATE / TIME — Current date and running timecode

✏️ 2. Entering Values — Tap Any Field to Write It

Every value on the board can be rewritten in place — each editable field is framed by a light gray outline. The outlines are a screen-only guide: captured snapshots always show a clean slate.

  1. Tap the value you want to change (for example the SCENE number).
  2. Type with the keyboard, or write with an Apple Pencil — iPadOS converts your handwriting to text automatically in the same field (see Apple Pencil handwriting below).
  3. Press return, tap another field, or tap empty board space to commit the value to the slate.

TAKE is a counter, so it only accepts numbers. If you prefer to fill in several fields at once, tap Edit in the top-left corner to open the full editor page, then press Save.

💡 Tip: Values update instantly — what you see on the board is exactly what the snapshot at clap time will capture.

🔊 3. Recording a Take (Clap)

Tap the clapper stick at the top of the screen ("TAP TO CLAP"). A full-screen white stage rises: the striped stick splits into two blades that open on their left hinge and slam shut, with the clap sound, a haptic impact and one inverted frame at the moment of the clap. Each clap also:

  1. Freezes the current timecode and captures a clean snapshot image of the board (editing guides are never captured).
  2. Creates a shot log entry with scene, take, timecode, date, and the snapshot.
  3. Increments TAKE for the next shot.

To start over, tap the circular reset arrow (↺) in the top bar and confirm — TAKE returns to 1.

⏱️ 4. Timecode and Frame Rate

The slate displays a real-time timecode readout driven by your chosen frame rate (Settings → Timecode):

  • 23.976 / 24 fps — Cinema and film production
  • 25 / 50 fps — PAL broadcast standards
  • 29.97 / 30 / 60 fps — NTSC broadcast standards

Use Jam to Manual Timecode to sync the counter to a camera or recorder's start timecode.

📋 5. Shot History

Every clap is saved automatically. Tap the film.stack icon in the top bar to open Shot History as a full page:

  1. Shots appear as snapshot cards showing the board image captured at clap time, plus scene, take, timecode, and date. Each card shows the whole board — images are never cropped, in any orientation.
  2. Use the grid/list button at the top to switch to a compact text list — your choice is remembered (default can be set in Settings → Shot Log).
  3. Search by scene, take, timecode, or notes.
  4. Tap any shot to open its detail page — add notes and delete the shot there.
  5. Swipe a list row left to delete (asks for confirmation first).

Shots recorded before snapshots existed show a "No snapshot" placeholder.

📤 6. Exporting Shot Logs

  1. Open Shot History.
  2. Tap Export in the top-right corner and choose one of the two options:
    • CSV Only — the text log.
    • CSV + Board Images — the CSV plus every board snapshot of the current list, each a full-resolution PNG named by date, scene, and take.
  3. Share via AirDrop, Mail, Messages, or save to Files.

The CSV contains Date, Time, Production, Roll, Scene, Shot, Take, Director, Cameraman, Timecode, and Notes for every shot — ready for script supervision reports or post-production.

✍️ 7. Apple Pencil Handwriting

FilmSlate Pro turns your writing into the text shown on the slate — the way a real marker writes on a physical slate, but with clean, legible, machine-readable results.

  1. Tap any board value to open its input field.
  2. Write with your Apple Pencil directly over the field. iPadOS Scribble converts the handwriting to text in place.
  3. Press return or tap elsewhere to commit.
💡 Tip: Scribble is an iPadOS system feature. Make sure it is enabled in Settings → Apple Pencil → Scribble on your iPad, and choose your writing languages there (English, Chinese, and many more are supported). Handwriting recognition happens entirely on your device — nothing is uploaded.

⚙️ 8. Customizing Settings

Open Settings (gear icon) — it opens as its own page with a back button:

  • Slate Font Size — Small, Medium, or Large (Large is very large; the slate auto-fits the screen at any size)
  • Board Display — Invert Board Colors switches the slate between white-on-black and black-on-white
  • Shot Log — Choose whether Shot History opens in Snapshots or Text mode by default
  • Timecode — Frame rate and jam sync
  • Appearance — Dark (recommended on set), Light, or Follow System
  • On Set — Keep Screen Awake stops the iPad from auto-locking while the slate is up
  • Help & Support — Replay the welcome guide / usage overlay, Privacy Policy, Support, Share
  • About — Version info, feature overview, and rate the app

Frequently Asked Questions

What is FilmSlate Pro?

FilmSlate Pro is a digital clapperboard app for iPad designed for film, television, and video production. It replaces a physical slate with a feature-rich digital alternative that records shot metadata, captures a board snapshot on every take, and exports production logs.

Does FilmSlate Pro require an internet connection?

No. FilmSlate Pro works completely offline. All features — slate display, handwriting input, clap recording, snapshots, shot logging, and CSV export — work without an internet connection, which makes it ideal for on-set use.

Does FilmSlate Pro require any device permissions?

No. FilmSlate Pro does not request access to your photo library, microphone, camera, contacts, or location. Exports use the standard iOS share sheet only when you choose to share something.

What devices does FilmSlate Pro support?

FilmSlate Pro is designed for iPad and requires iPadOS 16.0 or later. Apple Pencil handwriting is available on any iPad that supports Apple Pencil with Scribble enabled.

Where are my shot logs and snapshots stored?

All shot logs, board snapshots, and preferences are stored locally on your device (Core Data, UserDefaults, and the app's own folder for snapshot images). Nothing is transmitted to external servers. Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

Can I export my shot logs for use in post-production?

Yes. From Shot History, tap Export and choose CSV Only for the text log, or CSV + Board Images to also get every board snapshot as a full-resolution PNG named by date, scene, and take. The CSV contains Date, Time, Production, Roll, Scene, Shot, Take, Director, Cameraman, Timecode, and Notes for every shot.

Does FilmSlate Pro collect any personal data?

No. FilmSlate Pro does not collect, store, or transmit any personal information. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no tracking. All data stays on your device. See our Privacy Policy for details.

How do I reset the take number?

Tap the circular reset arrow (↺) at the top of the main screen and confirm — the take counter returns to 1 and counts up from there on each clap.

Why do my older shots have no snapshot?

Snapshots are captured for shots recorded after the feature shipped. Older entries simply show a "No snapshot" placeholder — their scene, take, timecode, and notes are all still there.

The app crashed or isn't working properly. What should I do?

Try force-quitting and relaunching the app. If issues persist, please email us at daibou007@gmail.com with details about the problem and your device model & iPadOS version.
