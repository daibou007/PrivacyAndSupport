Privacy Policy — ProbabilityLab

ProbabilityLab is an offline probability and statistics tool for iPad. It has no account, no advertising and no analytics, and it does not collect personal data.

Last updated: 23 September 2026

The short version. Every distribution, test statistic, p-value and simulation in ProbabilityLab is computed on your device. Nothing about you or your work is transmitted to us, because there is nothing in the app that transmits it. The only network feature is the optional AI question parser — off by default — which sends the question text you paste to an endpoint you configure with your own API key.

1. Who we are

ProbabilityLab is published by Daibou007 Team ("we", "us"). This policy explains what happens to information when you use the app. It applies to the ProbabilityLab application and nothing else.

2. Information we collect

We collect no personal information. Specifically, ProbabilityLab does not:

  • create or require an account, a sign-in, or a profile;
  • collect your name, email address, phone number, or contacts;
  • collect location data of any kind;
  • collect device identifiers, advertising identifiers, or usage analytics;
  • contain advertising, marketing pop-ups, or third-party tracking SDKs;
  • use cookies or any equivalent tracking technology.

3. Information stored on your device

ProbabilityLab saves your work locally so the app is useful between sessions. This data stays in the app's own storage on your device and is never uploaded:

  • Library items — the distribution parameter sets, Monte Carlo simulation records (including the seed, so runs stay reproducible), datasets you have saved, and test configurations, together with your favorites. Stored in the app's Application Support directory.
  • Datasets you enter — the values you paste into Data Analysis while working, and any you save to the library.
  • Preferences — your appearance choice (light or dark), chart rendering options, decimal precision, default significance level, simulation defaults, and which guidance tours you have already seen.

You can delete any of this at any time from within the app: library items can be deleted individually or cleared entirely from Settings → Local Data, and deleting the app removes all of it.

4. The optional AI question parser

ProbabilityLab has one optional AI feature — the Problem Solver's AI Parse. It is off by default and does nothing until you enable it in Settings → AI Parsing and supply your own API key.

When you use it:

  • Only the question text you paste is sent, to the API endpoint you configured, using the API key you supplied. It goes to that provider, not to us.
  • Your API key is stored in the iOS Keychain, readable only by this app. It is never written to preferences, never logged, and never sent anywhere except that provider.
  • Your datasets, library items, saved work and every computed number are never transmitted. The parsing model never sees your data — it sees the question and nothing else.
  • No calculation depends on the model. Every number shown next to its reading is produced by the local engine, so a model error is always checkable.

The provider's own privacy policy governs what they do with the text you send them. We have no visibility into it and receive no copy of it.

5. Children's privacy

ProbabilityLab collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13. Because no data is collected, no verifiable parental consent is required under COPPA. The app presents no advertising and contains no social features or user-generated content sharing.

6. Your rights under the CCPA and similar laws

The California Consumer Privacy Act, the GDPR and comparable regimes grant rights of access, deletion, correction and opt-out of the sale of personal information. Because ProbabilityLab collects no personal information at all, there is nothing for us to disclose, delete, correct or sell. We do not sell or share personal information, and we have not done so in the preceding twelve months.

7. Data security

Your work is protected by the app sandbox and, where you have enabled it, by your device passcode and iOS data protection. API keys are stored in the Keychain with the AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly accessibility class, which keeps them off backups and off other devices.
