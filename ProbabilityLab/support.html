Common questions

The distribution curve looks cut off at the edges.

That is the display window, not the distribution. Each chart draws the region where the distribution has visible mass; tails beyond it are real but too small to see. Switch the view to CDF to read cumulative behaviour, or widen the interval with the sliders to see how P(a < X ≤ b) keeps growing.

Why doesn't my Monte Carlo frequency match the theoretical probability exactly?

Because it is a random sample, and the gap is the lesson: frequencies converge to the theoretical probability as trials accumulate — that is the law of large numbers the module demonstrates. Press Run again, or use +10k to add ten thousand trials and watch the gap shrink. Every run is seeded (shown under the controls), so the same seed reproduces the same run exactly.

The CLT histogram does not look normal for a small sample size.

Correct, and expected. How fast sample means turn normal depends on the population: the uniform is nearly normal at n = 5, while the skewed exponential and Beta(2, 8) need a larger n. Increase the sample size or keep drawing samples — the orange curve is the normal distribution the theorem predicts, and the readouts below compare the empirical mean and spread against μ and σ/√n.

My values will not paste into Data Analysis.

Values may be separated by commas, spaces, semicolons or newlines. Use a period as the decimal separator, and do not include thousand separators, units or percent signs — 1,234.5 reads as two values (1 and 234.5). Invalid tokens are dropped and reported rather than silently ignored; check the banner above the indicators for what was cleaned.

The test says "fail to reject" but my answer key says "significant".

Check, in order: the tails (two-tailed doubles the p-value of a one-tailed test), α (the app defaults to 0.05), and which test matches your assumptions — σ known uses Z, σ unknown uses t, unequal variances across two samples use the Welch two-sample t. The conclusion line states exactly what was computed; if the inputs differ from your problem's, edit them and the result updates immediately.

How do I turn on the AI question parser?

It is off by default. Go to Settings → AI Parsing, enable the feature, choose a provider or enter your own endpoint, and paste your own API key. You need an account with that provider; the key is stored in the iOS Keychain and is never sent anywhere except the endpoint you configure. Use "Test Connection" to confirm it works. Quick Parse, the offline parser, needs none of this and covers the standard textbook formats.

The AI misread my question.

That happens, and the app is built for it: the parsed parameters are listed before the answers, and every number is computed by the local engine. Re-word the question, correct the interpretation manually in the suggested module (the "Open in" button fills every parameter for you), or use Quick Parse, which handles the standard formats deterministically and offline.

How do I get my charts and results out of the app?

Every module has an Export button. Charts export as high-resolution PNG; the PDF report adds the model's formulas, the chart and the conclusions on one page. Both go straight to the iOS share sheet, so you can save to Files, mail them, or hand them to another app.

I deleted something. Can I get it back?

No — deletions are permanent, which is why every one of them is confirmed first and names what is about to go. Settings → Local Data clears history, favorite marks or everything, each with its own confirmation. If you have an iCloud or Finder backup from before the deletion, restoring the device or the app's data will bring it back.

How do I replay the welcome tour or a module's guide?

Settings → Guidance → "Replay welcome tour" or "Replay feature guides". The question-mark button in each module's toolbar replays that module's guide immediately.

Does ProbabilityLab work offline?

Yes, entirely — except for the optional AI Parse, which by definition sends your question to the endpoint you configured. Every distribution, test, interval and simulation is computed on your device with no network connection at all.

Is there a subscription or an in-app purchase?

No. ProbabilityLab is a one-time purchase. There is no account, no sign-in, no subscription and nothing to buy inside the app.

Reporting a problem

To help us fix something quickly, include:

  • your iPad model and iPadOS version;
  • the ProbabilityLab version, from Settings → About;
  • the module you were in and the exact parameters or text you entered, copied rather than retyped;
  • what you expected and what happened instead;
  • a screenshot, if the problem is visual.
