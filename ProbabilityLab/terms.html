4. Accuracy and limitations

ProbabilityLab computes distribution probabilities, test statistics, p-values and stochastic simulations using the algorithms described in its built-in user manual. Those algorithms have limits, which the app states where they apply:

  • Displayed curves are drawn over a finite window chosen to contain the distribution's visible mass. Tails beyond the window are real but not drawn, and the CDF switch shows cumulative behaviour instead.
  • Monte Carlo results are frequencies from random sampling with a stated seed. They converge to the theoretical probability as trials grow; a finite run differs from it, which is the phenomenon the module teaches.
  • Numerical results are computed in double precision and displayed to the decimal places you configure; they are accurate to that display precision, not exact.
  • Hypothesis-test conclusions are phrased for teaching. A correct procedure on inputs that do not satisfy the test's assumptions (independence, expected counts, approximate normality) is still meaningless — checking assumptions remains your job.

Verify important results independently. ProbabilityLab is provided as an educational aid. It is not a certified engineering, financial, medical, scientific or safety-critical calculation tool, and it must not be relied upon as one.
