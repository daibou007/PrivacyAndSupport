Common questions

An equation I typed was rejected.

GeoSpace parses first- and second-degree canonical equations: planes (x + y + z = 1), parametric lines (x = 1+2t, y = 3−t, z = 5t), spheres, cylinders, cones, ellipsoids, paraboloids and hyperboloids. The rejection message tells you exactly what was wrong — cross terms (xy, xz, yz) must be written in canonical form, exponents must be whole numbers 0–4, and tilted quadric axes must be rewritten with the variable of symmetry as z. Check the Examples list on the Equations page for the accepted shapes.

Why is an unbounded surface drawn with an edge?

Planes, cylinders, paraboloids and hyperboloids are infinite; the viewport draws a finite window chosen to contain the visible structure. The surface continues beyond the drawn edge — that boundary is a display limit, not part of the geometry.

Auxiliary lines look dashed. Are they broken?

No — the 6:4 dashed pattern is the academic convention for hidden lines. Segments the solid occludes from your current viewing direction are dashed; exposed segments are solid. Orbit the camera and you will see the pattern change as lines pass behind the solid.

The distance or angle I computed does not match my answer key.

Check, in order: which vertices the task used (the panel lists them — "Vertices used: A, B, …"), whether your convention matches (line angles are reported as the acute angle; line–plane angles as the angle between the line and its projection on the plane), and that your base points are not collinear, which would define no plane. Results are computed in double precision and shown to four decimals.

How do I change the projection?

The toolbar switch on any page with a viewport toggles Perspective (human-eye teaching view) and Orthographic (true-scale measurement view). You can also switch from the Workspace quick panel, and set the perspective field of view in Settings → 3D Scene.

How do I replay the welcome slides or the workspace tutorial?

Settings → Guidance → "Replay Welcome Slides" or "Replay Workspace Tutorial". "Reset Module Tips" makes each module's one-time tip appear again on your next visit.

How do I get my work out of the app?

Export renders the current camera angle as a high-resolution PNG, or builds a PDF report containing the viewport image, every scene equation with its classified surface type, and all measurement annotations. Both go straight to the iOS share sheet, so you can save to Files, mail them, or hand them to another app.

The Problem Parser missed part of my problem.

The parser is rule-based and offline: it recognizes solid keywords (cube, cuboid, tetrahedron, prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone, sphere, octahedron), dimension phrases ("edge length 4", "height 7", "radius 2.5"), and equations embedded in the text. Anything it finds is listed in the extraction report; treat its output as a starting point and refine parameters in Solid Modeling. It is not an AI model, so rewording into the standard textbook phrasing helps.

I deleted a saved scene. Can I get it back?

No — deletions are permanent, which is why every one of them is confirmed first and names what is about to go. Settings → Local Data clears all saved scenes with its own confirmation. If you have an iCloud or Finder backup from before the deletion, restoring the device or the app's data will bring it back.

Does GeoSpace work offline?

Yes, entirely — every solid, equation, auxiliary element, measurement, section and export is computed on your device with no network connection at all.

Is there a subscription or an in-app purchase?

No. GeoSpace is a one-time purchase. There is no account, no sign-in, no subscription and nothing to buy inside the app.

Reporting a problem

To help us fix something quickly, include:

  • your iPad model and iPadOS version;
  • the GeoSpace version, from Settings → About;
  • the module you were in and the exact equation, parameters or problem text you entered, copied rather than retyped;
  • what you expected and what happened instead;
  • a screenshot, if the problem is visual.
