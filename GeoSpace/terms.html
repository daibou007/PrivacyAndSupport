4. Accuracy and limitations

GeoSpace solves distances, angles and spatial relationships and renders analytic surfaces using the algorithms described in its built-in user manual. Those algorithms have limits, which the app states where they apply:

  • Equations are limited to first- and second-degree canonical quadrics without cross terms; tilted axes must be rewritten to a canonical orientation first, and the app tells you when this is needed.
  • Surfaces that are unbounded (planes, cylinders, paraboloids, hyperboloids) are drawn over a finite window chosen to contain their visible structure; the surface continues beyond the window.
  • Section areas are computed from the convex hull of the plane–mesh intersection and stated as such; sections of curved solids are therefore accurate to the mesh resolution, not exact.
  • Numerical results are computed in double precision and displayed to four decimal places; they are accurate to that display precision, not exact.
  • Relationship conclusions (parallel, perpendicular, intersecting, skew, coincident) are judged within a small numeric tolerance appropriate for teaching.

Verify important results independently. GeoSpace is provided as an educational aid. It is not a certified engineering, architectural, scientific or safety-critical calculation tool, and it must not be relied upon as one.
